## 3. What is left, precisely

1. **Registry overflow: 2,900 rows / 25.3 h = 0.0269 % of registry traffic.**
   The queue peak (7,176) is the ETW share cap (7,168), so this is a burst-size
   problem, not a steady rate problem.
2. **Delete shed: 3,357 rows = 0.71 % of the 472,991 admitted NameDelete rows.**
   The write filter does not cover deletes; they are the last sheddable class.
3. **No other loss.** Ring, minifilter pipeline, nose, WFP and registry sensor
   are all zero; no crash or restart for 25.3 h.

## 4. Options

| Option | Work | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Declare a small budget** | owner decision + record | closes at 0.026 % loss; measured 24 h rates ~2,750 queue + ~3,180 shed |
| **B. Filter non-critical NameDelete too** | source + E2 + deploy + 24 h | removes the 3,357 shed and frees share; may also cut the registry overflow; R1002 still sees the `.locked` create via the minifilter |
| **C. Increase the ETW share / queue** | queue constant or dynamic share + E2 + deploy + 24 h | targets the 2,900 registry bursts directly; 2x queue or a larger ETW share |
| **D. Registry coalescing** | detection-semantics change | attacks 10.8 M reg_create at the source; highest risk |

Recommendation: **A for an RC** (0.026 % is a declared, measured envelope), or
**B** if the owner wants one more attempt at zero. B is the narrowest change:
it uses the same rule audit as the write filter (no rule matches an arbitrary
delete; R1002 is create-based) and removes the last sheddable class.
