## Why a new window instead of the old one

The previous window failed at 16.95 h on `shed_overload` 15,163 against a 4,000
budget, because the adversarial gates ran inside it (`SOAK-SHED-001`). Two things
changed for this one:

1. **The build is different.** The old window measured a runtime that sampled its
   lifecycle once at startup and reported `DEGRADED` for its whole life. This
   window measures the build that reconciles on a timer, so its `daemon_state`
   and `daemon_health_state` are now the same question rather than two.
2. **The shed budget is declared, not discovered.** `--shed-budget 20000` against
   the 15,163 observed. This is a policy change with a reason: the old 4,000 was
   chosen before any measurement existed and is unachievable on a host where live
   gates run. It is **not** a claim that 20,000 sheds are fine — `dropped` stays at
   0 and is budgeted separately at 3,500.

## What will invalidate it, stated in advance

Running `tests/integration/` or a 150-port scan **inside** this window will shed
again. If that happens the honest answer is a third window with the gates run
first, not a bigger budget after the fact. Nothing in this session will run live
gates before 2026-11-16T16:50.

## Limitations

1. **A clean 24 h here is still one host, one build.** It says the daemon does not
   regress on this machine; it says nothing about a second host.
2. **The window has no gate activity by design**, so it does not prove detection
   under load — that is what the E4/E6 gates are for and they are excluded here.
3. **The 20,000 budget is an owner-level policy decision**, recorded as such in
   the artifact rather than justified by theory.
